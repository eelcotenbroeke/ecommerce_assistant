# CLAUDE.md

Personal price-optimisation assistant for Dutch online shopping. Streamlit app with a Dutch UI; all logic is in `app.py` (about 1,500 lines). It is the owner's personal tool, not a product for others.

## Goal (still being defined)

Paste a product URL, get the cheapest effective way to buy it in the Netherlands: total price including shipping, running promotions (1+1, 2e halve prijs, folder deals) and price per unit.

Direction under discussion, not final until the owner confirms: anchor on the product's EAN (bol and Amazon product pages expose it), look it up on price-comparison sites (a Beslist lookup by EAN found the best price in testing), check AH, Etos, Kruidvat and Jumbo for promos, and keep a watchlist for recurring purchases because promos are time-limited. Update 2026-11-18: from the Streamlit Community Cloud server, Beslist answers with HTTP 202 and a small page (the signature of an AWS WAF bot check) instead of the product page, so the Beslist route does not work from the deployed app. A bot check is a block: do not work around it (no browser emulation, no changed User-Agent, no proxies). Chosen alternative (owner, 2026-11-18): Google Shopping searched by EAN through SerpApi (`serp.py`, key in Secrets). First real answers seen 2026-11-19 (owner's raw-answer download, fixture `tests/data/serp_real_3474637283414.json`): Google Shopping does not match a barcode exactly. For EAN 3474637283414 it returned 40 loosely related shampoos and the right product (Kérastase Gloss Absolu, Celini.nl) was number 10; there is no barcode or GTIN in the answer. So the app never guesses the product: `serp.py` groups the results into candidate products, an optional *Productnaam* field ranks them (and settles the choice only on a clear match), and otherwise the screen shows a list and the owner taps his product; only then are the shops fetched (second search). The bare EAN is also unreliable as query: for EAN 4013594586856 Google Shopping returned nothing at all (`shopping_results_state: "Fully empty"`, fixture `tests/data/serp_real_4013594586856_empty.json`). So when the owner fills in *Productnaam*, Google is asked for that name (not the EAN); the screen shows the search term, and an empty answer for a bare EAN tells him to fill in the name. Staggered lookup (owner agreed 2026-11-19, built slice by slice): identify the product by EAN/GTIN first; if that gives nothing, by brand + MPN (manufacturer's article code, read from the shop's page; a shop's own SKU such as Zalando's or Wehkamp's only identifies the product at that shop); last by product name. Each rung that asks Google costs a SerpApi search, so the next rung is chosen from what is known about the product, not by trying all blindly. Slice A (built): `pageprobe.py` + `probe_page.py`, the expander "Test winkelpagina's" fetches pasted product links once (honest User-Agent, no SerpApi searches), reports per shop whether the page can be read (a refusal or bot check is a block: reported, never worked around) and which EAN, MPN, brand, name and SKU it holds, and offers a trimmed JSON download to build fixtures from. Next: slice B (parser per shop from real pages), slice C (the ladder itself). First probe results (owner, 2026-11-19, fixture `tests/data/probe_real_coolblue_dyson.json`): Coolblue is readable (JSON-LD has brand, name, the shop's own sku and an offer with price and availability; the manufacturer's code stands in a spec row "Fabrikantcode", which the parser now reads as MPN; no EAN was found in JSON-LD, microdata or spec rows, so the probe also lists barcodes found next to an "ean"/"gtin" label anywhere in the page code, as hints only). Zalando and Wehkamp answered HTTP 403: blocked from the cloud app, reported and not worked around; for such shops the name can still come from the pasted link itself. Re-run with the MPN fix (owner, 2026-11-19): Coolblue's page code holds no EAN at all. Checked on the sold-out Dyson V15 (937057) and the in-stock V8 (983437), also by searching the full page source in Chrome for ean/gtin/barcode/upc/isbn and any 13-digit number (only timestamps). For Coolblue the ladder therefore starts at brand + MPN (V15: 476575-01, V8: 626457-01), then the name. Member check (owner, 2026-11-19): the owner's baseline of trusted shops is the Thuiswinkel Waarborg member list (`thuiswinkel_leden.csv`, 1,169 members: slug and domain; his export, a snapshot). `members.py` matches an offer by the host of its link (Google's `/goto` links say nothing), then by a shop name that is a domain ("Kruidvat.nl"; "easycosmetic.DE" is not the Dutch member), then by a plain name against the domain's first label and the slug ("Trekpleister"; alias "Albert Heijn" -> ah.nl). Each offer shows a green "Thuiswinkel Waarborg" mark; a switch (default on) hides the other shops, the recommendation is made again among the shops left, bol and Amazon.nl always stay, and the screen says how many were hidden. Not a hard rule: Celini.nl and Flaconi are not members but are real shops, hence the switch. Categories (owner, 2026-11-19): the member list has no category field, only a free-text description on each profile page, so `thuiswinkel_leden.csv` has a `categorie` column (at most two codes joined by "|") derived by keyword rules in `categories.py` from that text (1,174 profile pages read once through the owner's Chrome, 0.6 s apart; robots.txt allows /leden/; the descriptions are the members' own texts and are not kept in the repo). `categorize_members.py <omschrijvingen.json>` rewrites the column from a new export. It is a rough first guess: 107 of the 1,169 members stay "overig", the big generalists (bol, Wehkamp, HEMA) are "algemeen", and big chains can get an odd second category. `MemberList.categories_of/in_category` read it. Not on the screen yet: the dropdown to limit a search to sensible shops is still to be decided with the owner. Second probe round (owner, 2026-11-19, eight big shops, one product each, fixture `tests/data/probe_real_big_shops.json`): Amazon.nl and HEMA are readable and give the EAN (Amazon from the "Global Trade Identification Number" spec row, HEMA from JSON-LD `gtin13` per variant); bol, Kruidvat, Albert Heijn and MediaMarkt answer HTTP 403 (blocked, not worked around); Etos and Jumbo gave no answer (timeouts, no verdict). So direct shop pages are a minority route and Google Shopping stays the main one. Amazon links are therefore fetched now (`lookup._ean_from_amazon`: the product's own `/dp/<ASIN>` page on the pasted host, tracking and locale dropped, one request, no retry); a refusal or a bot-check page falls back to asking the owner for the EAN. A readable page without an EAN is normal for marketplace sellers (first live try 2026-11-19, B0DJR1RWS3, an FPD TV: only the ASIN and an item model number, no GTIN row; checked in Chrome): the lookup then searches Google on the page's own title (cut at " | ", at most 120 characters, `lookup._name_from_amazon_page`) or on the owner's *Productnaam* when he filled it in (his name wins); `source(None, steps, page_name=...)` is how a source gets that, Beslist cannot and asks for the EAN; the result then has no EAN, shows the search term and a note to check the product. Seen on two products (one with an EAN, one without); the search on a page title has only been tested with fakes. A name alone is enough (owner, 2026-11-19; real user test: for Kérastase Gloss Absolu the keyword search worked on Google Shopping and the app refused it): `lookup.name_from_input(text, hint)` returns the name when the link field is empty (the *Productnaam* field is then the name) or holds only words (no link, no barcode; *Productnaam* wins when both are filled in); a link or a barcode is never taken for a name, a link of an unknown shop stays "invalid". `lookup.lookup(..., by_name=True)` then calls `source(None, steps, page_name=None)` with no page read and no Amazon note; the serp source searches on the name as it does for an Amazon page without EAN (budget guard counts both steps, a vague name gives the candidate list with its own intro `lookup_view.choose_intro`, which does not talk about an EAN). Without SERPAPI_KEY a name alone gets `NAME_NEEDS_GOOGLE` (Beslist cannot search on a name, nothing is requested). `ean_page.LOG_LIMIT` is 60 so one session of ~25 products keeps all raw answers in the download. Not tested live yet (fakes only). Testrun (owner, 2026-11-19; list of 20 products in the Project doc q16, plan in q15): the expander "Testrun (vergelijking met Google Shopping)" on the EAN screen (only with a SERPAPI_KEY) takes a table pasted from Excel (columns nr, categorie, url, zoekwoorden, product; `benchmark.parse_table`) and runs every row twice, first all keyword runs (they carry the price test), then the link runs (they measure the front door). `product` is ground truth and is never searched: when the lookup returns a list of candidates the run taps the candidate that matches it best (`benchmark.pick_candidate`, a weak match is kept but marked `twijfel`), exactly what a user who knows the product would do. One run per script run with `st.rerun()` in between, so the owner can Stop and the table grows while it runs; one broken run becomes a record with status "error". `benchmark.record` writes one flat record per run (status, search term, candidate list, the tap, all offers with shipping known or unknown, cheapest total with unknown shipping counted as 0 and with known shipping only, the recommendation, the raw SerpApi answers of that run, searches used) and the download `testrun-resultaten.json` holds them with the rules (even nr = verdict set, odd = development). Comparing with the first Google Shopping page and with the store list behind the product is done afterwards from that file, not in the app. The run does not use the 15-minute cache. Tested with fakes only. Link to name (owner, 2026-11-19, "just build it"; direction: a user stands on a shop's product page and pastes its link): `linkname.name_from_link(url)` reads the words of the product slug in a link (the path segment with the most real words; ids, barcodes and a short leading page number are dropped; folders such as nl, p, producten lose to the slug; the query string is ignored; fewer than two real words gives None), with no request to the shop, so it also works for shops that answer 403 (bol, Kruidvat, AH). `lookup.lookup` uses it when the page cannot be used (bol refused or has no EAN, a shop the app does not know, an Amazon page with neither EAN nor title) and the source can search on a name (SerpApi; Beslist cannot): bol is still asked once first and the block stays in the steps; the result shows the search term and `NAME_FROM_LINK_NOTE`; a link with no name in it gets `NO_NAME_IN_LINK` (fill in the Productnaam). An EAN from a page or a title from an Amazon page beats the words of the link. The rules are general, not written for one shop; the 20 links of the first benchmark list are development material, so the verdict on this front door needs fresh links from shops not in that list. Not tested live yet (fakes and the 20 links read by eye). Benchmark searches: a link whose words hold a name now costs 2 SerpApi searches (`benchmark.max_searches`). Testrun on keywords (owner, 2026-11-19, 20 runs, `claude/q17_testrun_keywords_results_2026-11-19.md` in the Project): no price verdict possible (one clear match in ten verdict rows), but the store list shows only the first 13 shops (a `stores_next_page_token` was in 5 of 20 answers), the trust filter left nothing in 8 of 20, auto-pick on a name was wrong 2 of 2 and Google splits one product over several candidates. Direction (2026-11-19, owner: "take the lead", question: a consumer with a product link wants to know if there is a better deal): the value is a verdict relative to the shop the link comes from, on totals with shipping, for the same product, at shops he trusts. First slice built: `verdict.py` finds the link's shop among the offers (`LookupResult.reference_link` is the pasted link, kept through `finish_choice`; a shop is recognised by the host of the link: "Kruidvat.nl", "bol.com", "Amazon.nl - Retail", alias "Albert Heijn"), compares its total with the cheapest offer (the owner's free delivery from bol/Amazon applied; a saving under EUR 0.50 counts as "you are already at the best price"; the preferred pick is mentioned separately when it also saves money) and gives `BEST`, `CHEAPER`, `GONE` (link shop cannot deliver) or `UNKNOWN`; for `UNKNOWN` the screen asks for the total he sees on the page (`verdict.parse_total`, key `ean_typed_total`, forgotten at the next search) and compares that. The verdict is computed on all offers (the link's shop need not be a member) against the recommendation after the trust filter. Variant warning built (`variant.py`): sizes (ml, cl, l, g, kg) and pack counts (3x, x3, 10x395 gr, 6 stuks, duopack; 100 or more is pieces, not a pack) are read from what was asked for (the typed or link-derived search term, or the slug of the pasted link) and from the product title that was found; the screen warns "Let op: je zocht 400 ml, het gevonden product noemt 200 ml" only when both state something and differ (precision before recall: a title without a size is never a mismatch; a set that holds the size asked for is not flagged). Checked on the 20 development and verdict pairs of the keyword Testrun by eye; the offers' own titles are not compared (Google groups the store list per product). Not built yet: following the next page of shops, an empty-screen fix for the trust filter, no auto-pick. The goal and research documents live in the claude.ai Project "ecommerce_assitant", not in this repo.

Confirmed by the owner (2026-11-18): he buys online. Delivery from bol and Amazon is free for him, so those shops have effective shipping 0. He trusts bol and Amazon most: when one of them is less than 10% above the cheapest total, recommend it instead of the cheapest. This is implemented in `choice.py`.

## Commands

```bash
pip install -r requirements.txt pytest ruff
streamlit run app.py
pytest -q
ruff check --select E9,F63,F7,F82 .
```

CI runs the last two. Run them before opening a PR.

`python probe_beslist.py [EAN]` is a live check (two requests to Beslist, honest User-Agent): run it on the machine that will host the app to see whether Beslist can be read without a browser. It is not part of CI and the Claude sandbox cannot reach Beslist.

## Secrets

Read through `st.secrets`: `SERPAPI_KEY`, `BOL_CLIENT_ID`, `BOL_CLIENT_SECRET`. Optional: `SERPAPI_MONTHLY_LIMIT` (searches per month the phone screen may use; default 250, the owner's maximum: 100 on 2026-11-18, raised to 250 the same evening). Locally in `.streamlit/secrets.toml` (git-ignored). Never commit keys or paste them into PRs, issues or logs.

## Workflow

- Never push to `main`. Work on a branch named `claude/<topic>` and open a pull request.
- The owner has authorised Claude to merge its own pull requests (squash) once `ci` is green; no further approval is needed (instruction of 2026-11-18). Use auto-merge when the `main` ruleset allows it, otherwise merge through the REST API after checking that `ci` passed on the PR's latest commit. Never merge with `ci` failing or missing, and only merge PRs Claude opened. Branches are deleted after merge.
- Tooling: `gh pr create/view/merge` do not work in Claude sessions (GraphQL is blocked). Use the REST API through `gh api repos/{owner}/{repo}/pulls...`. Auto-merge is armed with `PUT /repos/{owner}/{repo}/pulls/{n}/ccr/auto_merge`. Deleting remote branches is blocked, so rely on delete-branch-on-merge.
- Keep PRs small and single-purpose. In the description say what you tested and what you could not test.
- The required status check is `ci`. Do not weaken or skip checks to get green.
- Do not add dependencies without explaining why in the PR.
- Prices from shops are snapshots. Never present a price or promo as current unless it was read from the live page in this session, and say when it was read.

## Code conventions

- User-facing strings are Dutch. Identifiers and comments mix Dutch and English: follow the surrounding style.
- New logic goes into small pure modules next to `app.py` (for example `ean.py`), each with tests in `tests/`; `app.py` only wires them in. No network or Streamlit calls inside the pure modules.
- Keep pure helpers (`parse_price`, `extract_volume_ml`, ...) free of Streamlit calls so they stay testable. `import app` works in tests because Streamlit runs in bare mode.
- Prices are floats in EUR, formatted with `fmt_eur`.
- `app.py` has CRLF line endings. Edit it without converting them and check `git diff --stat` before committing: a whole-file diff means the line endings were lost.
- The owner uses the app mainly on his phone. `app.py` opens on the EAN screen (`ean_page.py`, texts and rows from `lookup_view.py`, chain in `lookup.py`); the old search sits behind the "Zoeken (oud)" toggle. The offers are a list of blocks (shop, total, price/shipping/delivery, the shop's own title and details, a link to the shop; the recommended one first), not a table. The input and the search button are one `st.form` (Enter on the phone keyboard searches, and the text is read only on submit); each result says which EAN was searched and which product the source found. When Google returns several products and the optional *Productnaam* field does not settle it, the screen shows a list of candidate products (`Choose` result in `lookup.py`, `serp.candidates/rank/auto_pick`) and the shops are fetched only after the owner taps one (`lookup.finish_choice`); "Ander product kiezen" goes back to the list without a new search. `ean_page.render()` returns at once without a Streamlit session, because a form created during `import app` in the tests leaks into the next AppTest ("Forms cannot be nested"). `?url=<bol link or EAN>` runs the lookup straight away. All fetching goes through `fetching.py` (honest User-Agent); if a shop blocks it, report that and stop, never work around it.

## Known issues (assessment of 2026-11-17)

- SerpApi route (verified against real answers 2026-11-19): a lookup is 1 search for the candidate list plus 1 for the chosen product's shops (2 when the product name or a single candidate settles the choice at once). The owner accepts at most 250 searches a month (the counter is account-wide, old search and tests included): the screen reads the month's usage from SerpApi's free Account API (`budget.py`), checks the budget before each step, caches worked lookups and candidate lists for 15 minutes per session and counts calls. The store list (`google_immersive_product`, `more_stores=true`) is rich: link (some are relative `/goto?url=...`, resolved to `https://www.google.com/goto?...`), title (often with a promo prefix such as "2 voor 10.00:", "Bonus 2e gratis:", "1+1 gratis:", "2+3 gratis:", "47% korting |", which becomes "Actie: ..." in the details), rating, `tag`, `details_and_offers`, price and shipping. Shipping is missing for some shops (AH, Kruidvat, Trekpleister, Leef in the fixture): shown as "onbekend", never guessed. Not verified: whether the `/goto` links open the shop, and how well a search on the product name works in practice (built 2026-11-19, tested only with fakes).
- Promos are only shown as text ("Actie: ..."); they are not in the effective total or the price per unit yet.
- The `google_product` deep scan is dead (Google shut the service down; SerpApi points to the Immersive Product API). Not migrated.
- `google_shopping` results have no direct merchant link (confirmed 2026-11-19: only `product_link` to Google), so the page-level promo check cannot run from them; the store list has the links.
- Shipping is mostly guessed. The volume parser has no grams/ounces and misses "1,5 L x 6" and "duopack".
- The bol API integration has not been tested against the live API.
